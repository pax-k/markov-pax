I interpreted “novel” as emerging or underused application areas, not standard examples like weather, board games, PageRank, or simple inventory models.

A Markov-chain approach is useful when the real problem can be represented as states + probabilistic transitions + decisions or costs. For hidden states use an HMM; for controllable actions use an MDP/POMDP; for duration-dependent systems use semi-Markov models; for sampling huge spaces use MCMC/Markov state models.

High-potential real-world areas

Area	Real-world problem	Markov formulation	Why it is novel / useful
1. Emergency department capacity and bed allocation	ED crowding, bed shortages, patient triage, ambulance-arrival surges.	States: patient acuity queues, bed occupancy, service capacity. Actions: open beds, reassign staff, divert/transfer patients.	A 2025 Scientific Reports paper used a Markov/queueing optimization model for two-class ED patient queues and reported large reductions in waiting time and queue length while optimizing beds and servers. This is practical because hospitals need explainable operational rules, not only black-box forecasts.  ￼
2. Disaster-resilient food and medicine supply chains	Natural disasters interrupt warehouses, routes, cold chains, and last-mile delivery.	States: facility status, inventory level, route availability, demand zones. Actions: reroute, rebuild, open alternate depots, ration supplies.	A 2025 supply-chain study used an MDP for food supply-chain resilience under disasters, including facility reconstruction, transport routing, product flow, cost, response time, and equity of food access. Its Qatar flood case reported up to 95% response-time cost reduction, 9% resilience improvement, and over 99.5% food accessibility under severe disruptions.  ￼
3. Wildfire spread and evacuation triggers	Communities near wildland–urban interfaces need probabilistic warnings, not single deterministic fire maps.	States: grid cells classified by fuel, slope, wind, moisture, burning status, containment status. Transitions: probability a fire moves cell-to-cell. Actions: evacuation trigger, road closure, resource deployment.	Recent wildfire work combines stochastic cellular automata with reinforcement learning to identify critical fire trajectories that could reach communities, validated on synthetic and real-world case studies. A Markov-style transition model can turn “where is the fire now?” into “which evacuation trigger minimizes expected casualties and false alarms?”  ￼
4. Renewable microgrid reliability	Solar/wind-heavy microgrids face stochastic outages, weather variation, storage limits, and uncertain demand.	States: generator up/down/derated, battery state, weather regime, load level. Transitions: weather and component reliability. Actions: dispatch, storage use, backup activation, maintenance.	A 2024 study used a Markov reliability process to compare 20 microgrid configurations and evaluate failure rate, repair time, unavailability, expected energy not supplied, and loss-of-load probability. This is useful for villages, campuses, mines, islands, and hospitals running hybrid energy systems.  ￼
5. Bridge, building, and infrastructure deterioration	Owners need to decide when to inspect, repair, rehabilitate, or replace assets before failure.	States: condition grades from “good” to “failed.” Transitions: deterioration probabilities learned from inspections, weather, load, age, and maintenance history.	Markov and semi-Markov models are well suited because infrastructure naturally moves through condition states. Recent work applies Markov chains to service-life prediction, while bridge deterioration studies use large inspection datasets and semi-Markov processes to model time-dependent reliability and maintenance planning.  ￼
6. Water quality, drought, and urban water demand	Cities and river-basin managers need early warning for pollution, algae, turbidity, drought class, and short-term water demand.	States: latent water-quality regimes, drought classes, demand bands, pollution event stages. Transitions: seasonal, hydrological, weather-driven probabilities.	A 2025 water-quality model used an HMM to uncover latent state transitions in water-quality indicators and combined it with graph convolution over monitoring stations. A 2024 Tehran water-demand study used a Markov-chain model and reported better forecasting accuracy than ANN baselines in that case.  ￼
7. Urban expansion and land-use planning	Cities need to forecast sprawl, vegetation loss, heat-island risk, and land-cover transitions under policy scenarios.	States: land-use classes such as built-up, vegetation, barren land, water. Transitions: learned from satellite/GIS time series.	CA-Markov models are already used in land-use work, but the underexploited opportunity is policy simulation: zoning change, transit expansion, flood restrictions, conservation buffers. A 2025 Scientific Reports study used CA-Markov and GIS to evaluate Lahore’s 1994–2024 land-cover changes and forecast 2034/2044 trends.  ￼
8. Cybersecurity attack-chain prediction and moving-target defense	Security teams need to predict the next attacker move and choose defensive actions before compromise escalates.	States: MITRE ATT&CK tactics/techniques, host-level attacker actions, privilege level, lateral movement stage. Actions: isolate host, rotate IPs, deploy honeypots, revoke credentials.	MITRE ATT&CK is explicitly based on real-world adversary tactics and techniques; Markov/HMM models can estimate likely next states from incomplete evidence. A 2024 ScienceDirect paper used HMM for real-time host-level attacker next-action prediction and reported 95.66% accuracy in its experimental setup. A 2025 stochastic-game moving-target defense study reported higher attack-diversion and capture rates than baseline honeypot architectures.  ￼
9. Circular economy and EV battery lifecycle routing	Decide whether used batteries should remain in first-life use, go to second-life storage, remanufacturing, recycling, or disposal.	States: battery health bands, ownership stage, logistics stage, regulatory compliance state. Transitions: degradation, failure, resale, recovery, recycling yield.	A 2025 circular supply-chain study for EV batteries explicitly integrates Markov chains to model battery health and guide reuse, remanufacturing, and recycling decisions. This is a strong area because the product lifecycle is literally a state-transition system.  ￼
10. Manufacturing rework, scrap, and predictive maintenance	Factories need to reduce bottlenecks, rework, downtime, spare-part shortages, and defect propagation.	States: machine condition, process stage, inspection result, rework loop, scrap, finished-good state. Actions: inspect, repair, slow line, change allocation, replenish parts.	A 2025 metallurgical machining study combined Markov chains and queueing theory to model milling, drilling, reaming, packing, rework, inspection, and waste as probabilistic states. Another 2025 study used a Markov-based framework for IoT-driven maintenance and spare-parts planning in cement production.  ￼
11. Drug discovery and protein dynamics	Search chemical space, find binding modes, model protein conformational transitions, estimate binding kinetics.	States: molecular graph candidates, protein conformations, ligand-binding modes. Transitions: molecular edits, conformational jumps, binding/unbinding moves.	MCMC and Markov state models are natural tools for huge molecular spaces. MARS uses MCMC over molecular graph edits for multi-objective drug discovery, and recent work uses nonequilibrium candidate Monte Carlo to identify fragment binding sites and binding modes without prior pose knowledge. Newer Markov-state-model emulators aim to accelerate molecular-dynamics sampling.  ￼
12. AI-agent runtime safety	Autonomous LLM agents make stochastic tool calls, update memory, and interact with changing environments; operators need enforceable safety constraints.	States: task phase, tool state, memory state, environment state, risk state. Transitions: probabilistic next action/tool call. Actions: allow, block, redirect, request confirmation, rollback.	This is the most speculative but arguably most novel area. A 2025 paper proposes runtime enforcement of LLM-agent safety via probabilistic model checking, explicitly modeling LLM-agent transitions as probabilistic processes shaped by token sampling, memory/prompt history, and dynamic environments.  ￼

Best bets for “novel but practical”

The strongest near-term areas are hospital capacity, disaster logistics, wildfire evacuation, cyber attack-chain prediction, EV battery circularity, and water-system forecasting. They share three features: discrete operational states, measurable transition data, and decisions with clear costs.

The most technically novel areas are AI-agent safety, drug discovery/protein dynamics, and cyber moving-target defense. These are less “simple Markov chain” and more HMM/MDP/Markov-game/MCMC territory.

Important caveat

A first-order, time-homogeneous Markov chain is often too restrictive. If the transition probability changes with season, age, regulation, attacker adaptation, or time-in-state, use a non-homogeneous Markov chain, semi-Markov model, HMM, POMDP, or Markov model hybridized with ML. Bridge-deterioration research explicitly notes that basic Markov chains can be limited by memoryless and homogeneous assumptions, which is why semi-Markov models are often used for aging assets.  ￼
